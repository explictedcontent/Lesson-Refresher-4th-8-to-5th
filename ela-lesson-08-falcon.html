Reading Passage

The Skyscraper Falcon

High on a narrow ledge of the tallest skyscraper in the city, a young peregrine falcon named Pip peered over the edge. Her eyrie was not a rocky cliff like the ones her ancestors had used for thousands of years. It was a slab of cold concrete, hundreds of feet above the busy streets.

Pip had never flown. Far below, pigeons swirled and cars crawled like tiny beetles. Watching them made her feel anxious, and her small heart thumped fast.

Her mother, Vela, landed beside her with a rush of wind. "Today is the day, Pip," she said gently. "Today you fly."

Pip pressed herself back against the wall. She was reluctant, and the ground looked impossibly far away. "What if I fall?" she whispered.

Vela looked out over the glittering city. "Little one, falling and flying start the very same way," she said. "The difference is that a flyer spreads her wings. But first, you must commit. Halfway is the only way to truly fall."

Pip's talons trembled. Every part of her wanted to stay safe on the ledge. But she remembered that flight was in her blood — an instinct older than the city itself. She took one shaky breath. And she leaped.

For one terrifying moment she dropped, tumbling toward the street. The wind screamed past her. Then, deep inside, something ancient took over. Pip snapped open her wings — and the wind caught her. She stopped falling. She soared.

"I'm flying!" she cried. The whole city spread out beneath her like a map. Then Pip tucked her wings tight against her body and pointed her beak downward into a stoop — the falcon's famous dive. The buildings rushed up to meet her, faster and faster, the wind roaring in her ears. It was the most thrilling feeling she had ever known.

As she pulled up and looped between the towers, Pip finally understood. The skyscraper was her cliff. The city was her canyon of glass and steel. She belonged here, in the sky, just as surely as her ancestors had belonged to the mountains. Her fear began to transform into something new: confidence.

At last she swept back up to the ledge and landed beside her mother, breathing hard and grinning a falcon's grin. She felt triumphant — no longer a frightened nestling, but a true flyer of the skies.

"You were afraid," Vela said softly, "and you flew anyway. That is the bravest thing there is."

Comprehension Check

1. Where was Pip hatched? ▶
In an eyrie (nest) on a ledge of a city skyscraper, instead of a cliff like her ancestors used.
2. Why is Pip reluctant to fly at first? ▶
She's never flown, and the ground looks terrifyingly far below — she's afraid of falling.
3. What does Vela mean by "Falling and flying start the same way"? ▶
Both begin with a leap; the difference is committing and spreading your wings — you have to be brave to fly.
4. How does Pip feel by the end of the story? ▶
Triumphant and confident — her fear transformed into a feeling of belonging.
5. What is the THEME (big lesson) of the story? ▶
Courage means acting even when you are afraid.
