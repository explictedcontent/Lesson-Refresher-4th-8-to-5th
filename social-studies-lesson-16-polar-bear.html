The Top of the World

Read carefully — the quiz and activities come from this passage.

Far at the top of the world, around the North Pole, lies a frozen region called the Arctic. It is one of the coldest places on Earth. Here is something surprising: the Arctic is not really a continent at all. It is mostly a giant frozen ocean, ringed by the northern edges of the continents. That makes it the opposite of Antarctica at the South Pole, which is land surrounded by ocean. The Arctic is ocean surrounded by land.

Many countries touch the Arctic. Canada, the United States (through Alaska), Russia, Greenland (which belongs to Denmark), and Norway all have land in the far north. Together these are sometimes called the Arctic nations. Their coasts and the frozen sea between them are the home of the polar bear.

Arctic: the frozen region around the North Pole.   Indigenous: the first peoples of a place.   Sea ice: frozen ocean water.   Climate: the usual weather of a place over many years.   Conservation: protecting nature and wildlife.

Polar bears live only in the Arctic, roaming across the sea ice and coasts of these northern lands. They are never found near the South Pole. That is why polar bears and penguins never meet in the wild — polar bears live at the very top of the world, and penguins live near the very bottom!

People live in the Arctic too. Indigenous peoples like the Inuit have lived here for thousands of years. Traditionally they hunted and fished, and traveled by dog sled and kayak. They carry deep knowledge of the ice, the seasons, and the animals — knowledge passed down for many generations.

The polar bear is a powerful symbol of the Arctic. It appears on Canadian coins and in Inuit art and stories. But today the polar bear stands for something else too. The Arctic is warming faster than most of the planet, and the sea ice that polar bears need to hunt seals is shrinking. Because of this, the polar bear has become a well-known symbol of climate change.

Why should the rest of the world care about a faraway frozen ocean? Because the Arctic's ice helps keep the whole planet cool, like a giant mirror bouncing sunlight back into space. When that ice melts, it changes weather patterns far away. Protecting the Arctic — and its polar bears — is a job that connects people all around the globe.
