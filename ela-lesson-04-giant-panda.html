Phase 2 — Reading Passage (10 min)

The Gentle Giant of the Bamboo Forest

The giant panda is one of the world's most recognized animals — and one of the most carefully protected. With its distinctive black-and-white fur and round, gentle face, the giant panda has become a global symbol of conservation: the effort to protect nature and wildlife from harm.

Deep in the mountain forests of central China, where mist clings to bamboo groves and temperatures drop below freezing, giant pandas have carved out a unique home. These forests — once vast — have been reduced by farming and logging to isolated patches in three Chinese provinces: Sichuan, Shaanxi, and Gansu. Isolated means separated or cut off from others. Today, wild pandas exist in fewer than 30 fragmented habitat patches.

Perhaps the most surprising thing about giant pandas is their diet paradox. Pandas are classified as carnivores — meat-eaters — yet they eat bamboo almost exclusively. Their short digestive tracts, inherited from meat-eating ancestors, cannot process plant cellulose efficiently. To compensate — to make up for what's lacking — pandas eat for up to 14 hours daily, consuming 26–84 pounds of bamboo. This remarkable adaptation — a change that helps a living thing survive — is one of the most unusual in the animal kingdom.

The story of giant panda conservation is one of cautious hope. In 1990, fewer than 1,000 pandas survived in the wild — they were listed as endangered, meaning seriously at risk of dying out. Through international cooperation, habitat protection, and breeding programs, their numbers slowly recovered. By 2016, the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) officially changed their status to vulnerable — still at risk, but improving. Today approximately 1,800 live in the wild.

More than just a bear, the giant panda represents what's possible when humans choose to protect endangered species. China's network of 67 panda reserves, the dedication of conservationists worldwide, and decades of careful research turned a species teetering on extinction into a story of real progress.
