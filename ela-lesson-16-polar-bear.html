Reading Passage
10 min

Nanuk's Long Winter

Deep beneath the snow, inside a den so warm it felt like a secret little world, a polar bear cub named Nanuk spent his very first winter. He was curled up with his mother, Sila, and his small sister, listening to the wind moan far above. Outside, the Arctic was dark and frigid, and Nanuk was perfectly happy right where he was.

When pale spring light finally trickled into the den, Sila stretched and said, "It is time, little one. Time to learn to hunt." Nanuk was reluctant to leave the cozy den. Out there the ice was vast and the cold still bit hard, and he felt apprehensive about the whole frightening idea.

But Sila was patient. She showed him how to walk softly on the ice so his big paws made no sound, and how to trust his nose to find the seals hidden below. Nanuk's first tries were clumsy. He stomped too loudly, sniffed the wrong way, and scared every seal into the water. "I'll never get it," he grumbled, feeling frustrated.

"You will," said Sila. "Watch, and try again." Then one afternoon, a crack split open in the ice right beneath Nanuk's feet. He slipped, sliding toward the black, freezing water. Sila lunged and caught him by the scruff, pulling him back to safety. After that, Nanuk learned to tap the ice first and to be cautious near every dark edge.

For weeks Nanuk practiced. He grew taller and stronger, and slowly, surely, more capable. His mother never once gave up on him, even on the days when he wanted to give up on himself. She simply waited, and encouraged, and showed him again.

At last came the morning Nanuk had worked so hard for. He crouched beside a small breathing hole in the ice and held perfectly, perfectly still — the way Sila had taught him. Minutes crawled past. Then, in a sudden flash, he struck, and caught his very first seal. He was overjoyed, and bursting with a warm feeling of being truly proud.

As Nanuk sat in the bright Arctic sun, he understood something at last. It was his mother's patient teaching, and his own refusal to quit, that had carried him through the long winter and turned a tiny, frightened cub into a strong young bear. Patience, persistence, and a caring guide, he realized, are what help you grow.
