The Color-Changing Bug Catcher

A Reptile Through and Through

A chameleon is a reptile, just like a snake, a turtle, or a crocodile. That means it is cold-blooded, it has scaly skin, and it lays eggs. Most chameleons live up in the trees, where they climb slowly among the branches looking for their next meal.

How the Color Change Really Works

Everyone knows chameleons change color — but the reason is not what most people think. Their skin has layers of special cells packed with tiny crystals, called iridophores. When a chameleon relaxes or tightens its skin, those crystals move closer together or farther apart. That changes which colors of light bounce off the skin, so the chameleon looks green, then yellow, then bright orange. It is not like a mood ring, and it is not just for hiding.

Why Change Color at All?

The main reasons are temperature and talking. Because a chameleon is cold-blooded, it turns darker to soak up more heat when it is chilly, and lighter to cool down in the sun. Chameleons also change color to communicate — flashing bright colors to show off to a mate or to warn a rival to back off. Camouflage is just a bonus, not the main event.

Eyes That Move on Their Own

A chameleon's eyes are amazing: each eye rotates on its own, so one eye can look forward while the other looks backward. Together they give the chameleon nearly 360° vision to watch for predators and prey at the same time. The moment it spots a tasty bug, both eyes lock forward to judge exactly how far away it is.

The Rocket Tongue

Then comes the best trick of all. A chameleon has a ballistic tongue that launches out faster than a car speeding up. The tip is sticky and works like a tiny suction cup to grab an insect. Stretched out, the tongue can be longer than the chameleon's whole body — and it all happens faster than you can blink.

Grippy Feet and a Fifth Hand

To live in the trees, a chameleon needs a good grip. Its toes are fused into two bunches that face each other, like little mittens clamping onto a branch. Its tail is prehensile, which means it can curl and wrap around a branch like a fifth hand to hold on tight.

Slow and Sneaky

Chameleons move very slowly, and they even rock back and forth as they walk so they look like a leaf swaying in the wind. They are ambush hunters: they wait quietly, aim, and then fire that tongue by surprise. And because they are cold-blooded, they often bask in the sun to warm up — one more reason their color-change temperature control is so handy.
