Core Reading: The World's Fastest Traveler

Read carefully — the quiz and activities come from this passage.

The word peregrine means "wanderer" or "traveler" — and no bird earns that name better. The peregrine falcon is the most widespread bird of prey on Earth, found on every continent except Antarctica. From the frozen Arctic tundra to hot deserts to the middle of busy cities, this falcon has learned to live almost anywhere.

Peregrines are famous wanderers. Some travel up to 15,500 miles round-trip every year, flying all the way from the Arctic down to South America and back — one of the longest migrations of any bird. The Arctic-nesting peregrines that travel the farthest are sometimes called "tundra falcons."

Migration: a long seasonal journey animals make between homes.   Pesticide: a chemical sprayed to kill insects on crops.   Extinct: when a whole kind of animal has died out forever.   Endangered: at risk of dying out completely.   Falconry: the ancient art of training falcons to hunt.

But this amazing bird almost disappeared. In the 1950s to 1970s, farmers sprayed a pesticide called DDT on their crops. DDT built up in the food chain — bugs, then small birds, then the falcons that ate them. It made falcon eggshells so thin they cracked before the chicks could hatch. By 1970, peregrine falcons had vanished from the entire eastern United States. Many people feared they would become extinct.

Then people came together to save them. The United States banned DDT in 1972. Scientists — including a famous program at Cornell University — raised baby falcons in captivity and released them into the wild. Slowly, it worked. In 1999, peregrine falcons were removed from the endangered species list. It is remembered as one of the greatest conservation success stories ever.

Today, peregrines have a surprising new home: the city. They nest on skyscrapers, bridges, and cathedrals in places like New York, Chicago, and London. To a falcon, a tall building is just like a rocky cliff — and cities are full of pigeons to hunt. People long treasured these birds: for over 3,000 years, across Asia, the Middle East, and Europe, people trained falcons to hunt. This art is called falconry, and it is now on UNESCO's list of cultural heritage.
