🧊 Phase 2 — Counting Unit Cubes (10 min)

Volume is how much space is INSIDE a solid. Build prisms and count the cubes.

📋 Area is Flat. Volume is Full.

Area measures a flat surface — how many square units cover it. Nothing fits inside a flat shape, because a flat shape has no inside.

Volume measures a solid — how many cubic units fit inside it. A unit cube is a cube that is 1 unit long, 1 unit wide, and 1 unit tall. Its volume is exactly 1 cubic unit.

Length
how far across (1-D)
Area
square units (2-D, flat)
Volume
cubic units (3-D, solid)
The fastest way to count cubes: find ONE layer first, then multiply by the number of layers. If a layer holds 12 cubes and the prism is 3 layers tall, that is 12 × 3 = 36 cubic units — no need to count one cube at a time.

✖️ Phase 3 — The Volume Formula (12 min)

V = l × w × h — and how to work backwards to find a missing dimension.

📋 V = length × width × height

V = l × w × h

Why it works: length × width tells you how many cubes fit in one layer on the bottom. Multiplying by the height tells you how many layers there are. So the formula is really just "cubes in a layer × number of layers" — counting, done fast.

Step 1
l × w = one layer of cubes
Step 2
× h = all the layers
Step 3
label it cubic units
Labels matter. If the sides are in centimeters, the volume is in cubic centimeters (cm³). Inches give cubic inches (in³). Plain units give cubic units or units³. Writing "square cm" for a volume is wrong — squares are flat, cubes are solid.
Missing a dimension? Divide. If V = 24, l = 4, and w = 2, then one layer is 4 × 2 = 8 cubes, so the number of layers is 24 ÷ 8 = 3. The height is 3 units. Always check: 4 × 2 × 3 = 24 ✓
