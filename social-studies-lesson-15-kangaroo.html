The Land Down Under

Read carefully — the quiz and activities come from this passage.

Australia is one of the most unusual places on Earth. It is the only place that is a country, a whole continent, AND surrounded by ocean like a giant island, all at the same time. Because it sits far south of the equator, in the Southern Hemisphere, its seasons are flipped from ours. When it is winter in North America, it is summer in Australia — so Australians often have summer in December and go to the beach for the holidays!

Australia was separated from all the other land for millions of years. Cut off by the ocean, its plants and animals evolved on their own. That is why so many creatures live there and nowhere else on Earth. Many are marsupials — mammals like kangaroos, koalas, and wombats that carry their babies in a pouch. Australia is even home to the strange platypus, a mammal that lays eggs.

Continent: one of Earth's huge land areas.   Hemisphere: one half of the globe.   Marsupial: a mammal that raises its baby in a pouch.   Indigenous: the first people of a place.   Outback: Australia's dry inland country.

The kangaroo is a proud national symbol of Australia. It appears on the country's coat of arms — standing next to a large bird called the emu — and it shows up on coins and on the logo of the Qantas airline. People say the kangaroo and emu were chosen because neither animal can easily walk backward. They symbolize a nation that is always moving forward.

Long before any of that, the Aboriginal peoples of Australia were already there. They are the world's oldest continuous cultures, living on the land for more than 60,000 years. Kangaroos have always been important to them — as food, in their stories, and painted onto ancient rock art.

Kangaroos live all across Australia's grasslands and open bush — the vast, dry inland region called the outback. They often gather in groups called mobs. The outback is huge but has very few people. Most Australians instead live in big cities like Sydney and Melbourne, which sit along the cooler coast where the ocean is close.

Today kangaroos are so common that they appear on yellow road signs warning drivers to watch out for them. They are protected animals, but their numbers are also managed, because very large mobs can eat the grass that farmers need for their sheep and cattle. From ancient rock art to modern road signs, the kangaroo is stitched right into the story of Australia.
