Built for Speed

The Stoop

The peregrine falcon is the fastest animal on the entire planet. Its secret is a hunting dive called a stoop. When a peregrine spots prey below, it folds its wings in tight and drops out of the sky like a missile, reaching about 240 miles per hour. Nothing else alive moves that fast.

Why Is It So Fast?

Three things work together. First, the falcon has a teardrop (streamlined) body shape that lets air slip past instead of pushing back — this reduces drag. Second, its wings are stiff and pointed, so they fold back to cut through the air. Third, a peregrine is heavy for its size, so gravity pulls it down fast, like a dense arrow.

The Physics, Made Simple

You can think of the stoop as a race between two forces. Gravity pulls the falcon straight down toward the ground. Air pushes back against anything moving through it — that push is called air resistance, or drag. The falcon's smooth, pointed shape lets the air slip past with very little drag, so gravity wins and the bird keeps speeding up.

Breathing at 240 mph

At 240 miles per hour, the rushing air could actually damage a falcon's lungs. But peregrines have bony, cone-shaped bumps inside their nostrils, called tubercles. These act like little baffles that slow the air down before it reaches the lungs, so the falcon can still breathe. Engineers were so impressed that they studied these bumps to help design the air intakes on jet engines!

Super Vision

A peregrine's eyesight is about 3 times sharper than a human's — it can spot a pigeon from over a mile away. It can even see ultraviolet light. To protect its eyes in the dive, it has a third eyelid called a nictitating membrane — a see-through eyelid that wipes and shields the eye without blocking its view.

The Strike

Here is the clever part: a peregrine usually does not grab prey with its talons at full speed — a crash at 240 mph could hurt the falcon. Instead, it balls up a foot and punches the prey in mid-air. Then it loops back around to catch the stunned prey as it falls.

Adaptations Recap

Everything about the peregrine is built for the dive: a streamlined body, pointed wings, nostril baffles, sharp eyes, a third eyelid, and powerful talons. It is also a warm-blooded bird — covered in feathers and hatching from eggs, just like all birds, which are the living relatives of the dinosaurs and reptiles.

How Fast Is That, Really?

A cheetah is the fastest animal on land, running about 70 miles per hour. A peregrine's dive is 240 mph — more than 3 times faster than the fastest land animal. Nature's speed champion is a bird falling out of the sky.
