Lesson 1 — Place Value 🔢

0–5 min Warm-Up

1. In 47, what is the 4 worth?
2. In 283, what is the 8 worth?
3. In 615, what is the 6 worth?
Say it out loud too: read 309 ("three hundred nine") and 1,250 ("one thousand, two hundred fifty"). No typing — just say them.

5–15 min Teach: How place value works

Every digit has two things: the digit itself, and the place it sits in. The place tells you what it's worth. Each place to the left is 10× bigger than the one to its right.

Millions | Hund-Thous | Ten-Thous | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones
1,000,000   100,000     10,000      1,000        100      10      1

Example 1 — break 6,482 apart

DigitPlaceWorth
6Thousands6,000
4Hundreds400
8Tens80
2Ones2

Add them up: 6,000 + 400 + 80 + 2 = 6,482. ✅ That's expanded form.

Example 2 — 3,205,070

Watch the zeros — they hold the empty spots. We read it: "three million, two hundred five thousand, seventy." Read to a comma, say "million," read to the next comma, say "thousand," then the rest.

27–30 min Wrap-Up

Challenge: A 4-digit number where the thousands digit is 7, the tens digit is 2, and the hundreds and ones are both 0.

Then ask: "Tell me one thing you learned about place value today."
