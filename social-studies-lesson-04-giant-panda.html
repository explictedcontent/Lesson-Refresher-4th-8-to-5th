Phase 2 — Core Reading (10 min)

The Giant Panda's World

The giant panda is one of the most recognized animals on Earth — and one of the most carefully protected. Native only to a small region in central China, the giant panda has become a global symbol of conservation and international cooperation.

Where They Live: Wild giant pandas are found exclusively in China, in the provinces of Sichuan, Shaanxi, and Gansu. These areas contain dense bamboo forests on misty mountain slopes at elevations between 5,000 and 10,000 feet. The mountain ranges that shelter pandas include the Qinling, Minshan, and Qionglai ranges — rugged terrain where bamboo grows thick and predators are few.

What They Eat: Bamboo makes up 99% of the giant panda's diet. Because bamboo is low in nutrition, pandas must eat between 26 and 84 pounds of it every single day, spending up to 14 hours eating. This near-total dependence on one plant makes habitat loss especially dangerous — when bamboo forests disappear, pandas have nowhere to go.

Conservation Story: In the early 1990s, fewer than 1,000 giant pandas survived in the wild — they were listed as Critically Endangered. Through decades of effort by China and international partners, the population slowly recovered. By 2014, surveys counted 1,864 wild pandas. In 2016, the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) officially changed the panda's status from "Endangered" to "Vulnerable" — a rare conservation victory. China has established 67 protected nature reserves dedicated to panda habitat.

Panda Diplomacy: China has long used giant pandas as diplomatic gifts and loans to other countries — a practice called "panda diplomacy." When China lends a panda to a foreign zoo, it is considered a gesture of friendship and goodwill. The WWF (World Wildlife Fund) chose the panda as its logo in 1961, cementing the animal's role as the face of global conservation.
