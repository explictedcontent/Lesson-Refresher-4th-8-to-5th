📖 Phase 2 — Place Value & Reading Decimals (8 min)

Learn how decimal places work, then read decimals off a number line.

📋 The Decimal Point Splits Whole from Part

A decimal shows a number that is between two whole numbers. The decimal point separates the whole part (on the left) from the part smaller than 1 (on the right).

The first place after the point is tenths (one piece out of 10). The next place is hundredths (one piece out of 100). Here is the number 3.47:

Ones
3
3 wholes
.
Tenths
4
4 tenths
Hundredths
7
7 hundredths
Read "3.47" as "three and forty-seven hundredths."
A number line helps you SEE a decimal. Between 0 and 1 there are 10 equal steps — each one is one tenth (0.1). So a dot 4 steps past 0 sits at 0.4.

⚖️ Phase 3 — Comparing Decimals (10 min)

Which decimal is bigger? Watch out — a longer decimal is NOT always the larger one.

📋 Compare Place by Place — Tenths First

To compare two decimals, line them up and check the biggest place first:

Step 1
Compare the ones (whole part)
Step 2
If tied, compare the tenths
Step 3
If still tied, compare hundredths
Trap: is 0.7 or 0.68 bigger? 0.68 has more digits, but 0.7 = 7 tenths and 0.68 is only 6 tenths (plus a bit). 7 tenths beats 6 tenths, so 0.7 is greater! Tip: give them the same number of places → 0.70 vs 0.68, and 70 > 68.
