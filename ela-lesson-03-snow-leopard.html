🐆 The Ghost of the Mountains

The snow leopard is one of the most elusive big cats on Earth. Known as the "Ghost of the Mountains," this magnificent creature is adorned with a coat of pale gray fur covered in dark rosette spots — perfect camouflage among rocky mountain slopes.

Snow leopards are solitary animals, preferring to roam alone across the high peaks of Central Asia. They live in 12 countries, from Russia to India, often at elevations above 10,000 feet — higher than most other large predators ever go.

As an apex predator, the snow leopard sits at the top of its food chain. It hunts blue sheep, ibex, and hares, sometimes leaping more than 30 feet across cliff gaps in a single bound. Its thick, long tail — used as a rudder for balance when making sharp turns at speed — can be nearly as long as its entire body!

Despite being such a powerful hunter, the snow leopard is vulnerable to extinction. Fewer than 4,000 remain in the wild today. Habitat loss, poaching, and climate change are all shrinking its mountain home. Conservation teams across Asia and beyond are working hard to protect this ghostly, spotted mountain king.
