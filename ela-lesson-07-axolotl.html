Reading Passage

Axo and the Missing Leg

Axo was a young axolotl who lived in the cool, green canals of Xochimilco. Every morning he wove through the chinampa roots — the underwater tangles beneath the floating gardens — poking his pink nose into every shadowy gap. Nothing in the canals was safe from his exploring.

Then, one bright afternoon, a shadow fell across the water. A hungry heron struck! Its beak stabbed down like a spear. Axo twisted away just in time — but not all of him. When he stopped shaking, his back left leg was gone. Only a stinging wound remained.

Lupe, an older and wiser axolotl, found him hiding under a lily pad, trembling.

"It took my leg," Axo whispered. "I'll never explore again."

Lupe smiled her wide axolotl smile. "Little one, let me tell you the secret of our kind. We are the rebuilders. What we lose, we grow again. But you must be patient."

"Grow it… back?" Axo stared at her. "That's impossible."

"Watch and wait," said Lupe.

Weeks passed. First a tiny bud appeared where the leg had been. Axo checked it every hour. "It's too slow!" he groaned. He was impatient, wiggling and worrying.

"A seed does not sprout faster because you shout at it," Lupe said. "Your body knows how to transform that little bud into a leg. Give it time."

So Axo waited — but he did not sit still. With three legs, he learned to swim in a new sideways glide. He learned to hide deeper in the roots, and to hunt worms with a quick snap instead of a chase. Day by day, he became more resourceful than he had ever been with four legs.

On Day 50, Axo stretched out his new back left leg. It was identical to the old one — same size, same tiny toes, not even a scar.

"It's perfect!" he cheered. Then he grew quiet, thinking. "Lupe… the leg isn't the only thing that grew back, is it? I'm braver now. My courage grew too."

Lupe nodded. "That, little one, is resilience. What grows back can be stronger than what was lost."
