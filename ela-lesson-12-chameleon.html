2
Reading Passage
10 min

Kali Learns to Blend In

Kali had just moved to a new part of the forest, and she felt like she didn't belong. All around her, chameleons she had never met greeted each other, shifting their colors smoothly from green to gold to blue. Kali knew she was supposed to adapt to this new place, but she didn't know how. Whenever she got nervous, her skin flushed a dull, obvious brown. Instead of blending in, she felt conspicuous, as if a spotlight were pointing right at her.

She tried to copy the others, snapping her skin from color to color as fast as she could, but it only came out blotchy and strange. The harder she tried to look like everyone else, the more wrong she looked. "You're painting yourself brown again," said a calm, wrinkled chameleon resting on a branch nearby. "I'm Old Zuberi. Come, sit." Kali climbed up beside him, embarrassed. "The others make it look so easy," she mumbled. "I just want to fit in."

Zuberi chuckled softly. "Color is a language, little one. You are not hiding — you are speaking. When you turn brown from fear, you are shouting your worry to the whole forest. Let your colors be genuine, and they will say something true about you. Copying others will never work, because their colors are not your words."

Kali wanted to change instantly, right that second. But Zuberi shook his head. "Real change takes time and deliberate practice. You cannot rush it, and you cannot fake it." So Kali practiced, a little each day. Slowly, she learned to breathe and stay calm, and when she did, her colors stopped looking muddy and dull. Instead they turned vivid and confident, glowing with a warmth that felt like her own.

One afternoon a rival chameleon puffed himself up and flashed bright warning colors, trying to scare her away from the branch. Kali's heart pounded. The old Kali would have flushed brown and fled. But she remembered Zuberi's words. She stayed calm and let her colors answer clearly and steadily, without panic. Surprised, the rival slowly backed down and slipped away.

That was when Kali understood. She didn't need to copy everyone else to matter. Being authentic — showing who she really was — is what earned respect. By the end of the season, she finally felt at home. She belonged, not because she had blended in with the crowd, but because she had learned to be herself. Fitting in, she realized, was never about copying others. It was about being genuinely, truly you.
