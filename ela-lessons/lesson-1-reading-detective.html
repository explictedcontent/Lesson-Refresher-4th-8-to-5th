📖 Part 2 — The Reading 15 min

"Read this like a detective. Watch for clues — you'll need them!"

The Mystery of the Missing Lunch

Maya raced into the classroom, her stomach growling like a grumpy bear. All morning she had been thinking about the warm slice of pizza her dad had packed in her bright red lunchbox. But when she opened her cubby, her heart sank. The lunchbox was gone!

"Don't panic," Maya whispered to herself. "A good detective stays calm."

She looked around the room for clues. First, she noticed a small puddle of water near the cubbies. Then she spotted muddy paw prints leading toward the reading corner. Finally, she heard a soft crunching sound coming from behind the bookshelf.

Maya tiptoed over and peeked behind the shelf. There was Biscuit, the class guinea pig, happily nibbling on a piece of her pizza crust! His cage door hung wide open.

Maya laughed out loud. "Mystery solved!" she said. "Biscuit, you're the sneakiest little thief I've ever met."

She gently scooped up Biscuit and returned him to his cage, making sure to latch the door this time. Then she shared what was left of her lunch with her best friend, Leo, who had been saving her a seat all along.

🖍️ Interactive step: Read it again and underline the 3 clues Maya found (hint: third paragraph!). On screen, jot them in Part 3.

🔑 Answer Key (for the grown-up — click to open)

Part 2 — Three clues: (1) a small puddle of water near the cubbies, (2) muddy paw prints toward the reading corner, (3) a soft crunching sound behind the bookshelf.

Part 3:
1. Maya was worried her lunch / red lunchbox (the pizza her dad packed) was gone.
2. (1) the puddle, (2) the paw prints, (3) the crunching sound. (Seeing Biscuit is the solution, not a clue.)
3. Biscuit the guinea pig took it. Not really a "thief" — a thief steals on purpose, but Biscuit is an animal who got out and was hungry. (Accept reasoning either way if explained.)
4. Accept: "Don't panic," Maya whispered to herself. / "A good detective stays calm." / "She looked around the room for clues."
5. Open answer — look for: Maya is kind/generous/a good friend; Leo saved her a seat, so she's being nice back.

Part 4: 1=(b), 2=(c), 3=(a). Bonus sentence will vary.

Part 5: Answers vary — check each comparison uses "like" or "as."

Part 6: Score with the checklist. Strong paragraph = clear topic sentence, 2+ clues, a synonym, a simile attempt, and a solving sentence. Celebrate effort and creativity!
