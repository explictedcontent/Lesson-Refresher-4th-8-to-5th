📖 Part 2 — Mentor Story (Read Like a Writer) 10 min

"Writers learn by reading. Notice HOW this story is built — we'll copy the moves!"

The Last Day of Summer

Beginning: Tomás had been waiting all summer to ride the giant water slide at Splash Park. Today was finally the day — but it was also the very last day the park would be open before it closed forever.

Middle (the problem!): When Tomás reached the front of the line, the lifeguard held up her hand. "Sorry," she said. "You have to be this tall to ride." Tomás's heart dropped. He was one inch too short. His eyes stung, and the long walk back down the steps felt like a mile.

Then he had an idea. He remembered the thick-soled sneakers in his backpack. His hands trembled as he laced them up tight and climbed the steps again. He stood as straight as a flagpole next to the measuring stick.

End (solution!): The lifeguard smiled. "Just made it!" Tomás zoomed down the slide, wind rushing past his ears, laughing the whole way. It was the best last day of summer he could have imagined.

🔎 Notice the labels: every story has a Beginning, a Middle with a problem, and an End with a solution.

🔑 Teacher Notes & Answer Key (click to open)

Part 3 — Story Mountain (from "The Last Day of Summer"):
1. Tomás, at Splash Park on the last day it's open.
2. Problem: he is one inch too short to ride the slide.
3. Climax: he laces up his thick-soled sneakers and stands at the measuring stick again.
4. Solution: the extra height makes him just tall enough — "Just made it!"
5. Ending: he's thrilled/joyful — the best last day of summer.

Part 5 — Show, Don't Tell: 1=(b), 2=(a), 3=(b). The "tired" sentence will vary — look for actions/details (yawning, heavy eyelids, dragging feet) instead of the word "tired."

Parts 1, 4, 6: Open-ended. Use the Part 6 checklist to give feedback. Praise specific moves: a vivid detail, a clear problem, a satisfying ending. Aim for effort and voice over perfect spelling.

Extension idea: have the learner read their finished story aloud and add ONE more "show, don't tell" sentence.
