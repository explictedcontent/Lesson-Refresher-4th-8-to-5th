Reading Passage
10 min

Zuzu's First Flight

Zuzu clung to the edge of the hive's round doorway, her wings trembling. For her whole short life she had lived deep inside the dark, warm hive, doing small jobs — cleaning cells, feeding babies, packing pollen. Now, for the very first time, she was a forager. In front of her stretched the enormous, bright, buzzing world, and it felt completely overwhelming.

"You look frozen, little one," laughed a warm voice beside her. It was Mella, an older forager with dusty golden legs. "Every bee feels that way on her first flight. Follow the sun, little one, and watch how I dance when we return."

Zuzu took a breath and let go. The wind caught her wings, and suddenly she was flying! She followed Mella across a field bursting with color. Zuzu was diligent — she visited flower after flower, dipping into each one just the way she had practiced. Her legs grew heavy with golden pollen, and her honey stomach filled with sweet nectar. She lost track of time, working carefully, never rushing, never missing a bloom.

Then a sudden breeze pushed her sideways, and when Zuzu looked up, Mella was gone. The field looked different from every direction. Her heart pounded. She was lost! But then she remembered Mella's words: follow the sun. Bees have an instinct for this, a built-in sense. Zuzu found the sun, turned her body just so, and used it to navigate her way home.

Back at the hive, Zuzu watched Mella do something amazing. Mella wiggled her body and traced a looping path across the honeycomb — a waggle dance. At first it looked silly. Then Zuzu understood: the dance was a precise set of directions. The angle, the speed, the length — every wiggle told the other bees exactly where the best flowers were. The dance was a language!

Zuzu's own legs were still heavy with pollen from a patch of clover she had found. Her heart raced, but she stepped onto the honeycomb and tried her very first waggle dance. It was wobbly and shaky and far from perfect — but the other foragers leaned in, watching closely, and they understood. A moment later, a wave of bees streamed out the doorway toward the clover, all because of Zuzu.

Zuzu felt something warm spread through her. This was cooperation — every bee doing her part so the whole hive could thrive. Her small work mattered to everyone. At last she felt like a true member of the colony. She had found her place — not by being the biggest or the fastest, but by giving her share to something far bigger than herself.
