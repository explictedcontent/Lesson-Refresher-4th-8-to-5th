# Lesson 9: Balance & Symmetry

**Principle of Design:** Balance
**Time:** 30 minutes

## Learning goal
The student can create symmetrical (mirror) balance and asymmetrical (uneven but stable)
balance, and can explain the difference.

## Vocabulary
- **Balance** — how the visual "weight" is spread across an artwork.
- **Symmetrical balance** — both sides match like a mirror (a butterfly, a face).
- **Asymmetrical balance** — sides are different but still feel even (one big shape
  balanced by several small ones).
- **Radial balance** — design spreads out from a center point (a flower, a snowflake,
  a wheel).
- **Line of symmetry** — the line that divides matching halves.

## Materials
- White paper
- Pencil, markers or crayons
- Ruler (optional)

## Lesson plan (30 minutes)

**Warm-Up — 5 min**
Fold paper in half. On one side draw half of a simple symmetrical thing (half a heart,
half a butterfly) with the fold as the center. Try to copy it exactly on the other side.

**Concept / Mini-Lesson — 7 min**
Explain the three kinds of balance with examples from nature and objects:
- Symmetrical = butterfly, human face, a castle gate.
- Asymmetrical = a big tree on one side balanced by a small sun and birds on the other.
- Radial = sunflower, ferris wheel, mandala.
Stress that asymmetrical art is NOT "unbalanced" — it just uses different sizes/colors to
feel even.

**Guided Practice — 6 min**
Draw a small radial design: start with a dot in the center and add the same shape/petal
repeating around it (like spokes on a wheel).

**Main Activity — 10 min: "Three Kinds of Balance"**
Divide the paper into three sections and create one quick design for each: a symmetrical
design, an asymmetrical design, and a radial design. Color them so the balance reads
clearly.

**Reflection / Share — 2 min**
Which type was hardest to balance? Which is your favorite kind of balance?

## If You Finish Early (+10–15 min)
**Symmetry Mask or Mandala:** Design a face mask using perfect symmetrical balance, OR
build a detailed radial mandala from the center outward, adding a new ring of repeating
shapes each time you go around. Color it with a complementary palette from Lesson 3.

## What to look for (teacher check)
- The symmetrical halves genuinely mirror each other.
- Student can explain why an asymmetrical design still feels balanced.
