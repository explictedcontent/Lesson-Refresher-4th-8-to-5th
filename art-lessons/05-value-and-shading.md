# Lesson 5: Value & Shading — Light to Dark

**Element of Art:** Value
**Time:** 30 minutes

## Learning goal
The student can make a smooth value scale and use shading to make a drawing look
three-dimensional with a clear light source.

## Vocabulary
- **Value** — how light or dark something is.
- **Value scale** — a strip from white to black with grays in between.
- **Light source** — where the light is coming from.
- **Highlight** — the lightest spot, where light hits directly.
- **Core shadow** — the darkest part of the object, away from the light.
- **Cast shadow** — the dark shape an object throws onto the ground.
- **Blending / gradient** — a smooth change from light to dark.

## Materials
- Graphite pencil (a softer pencil like a 2B helps, but any pencil works)
- White paper
- A ball or round fruit (apple, orange) to observe
- A lamp or flashlight (phone light works)

## Lesson plan (30 minutes)

**Warm-Up — 5 min**
Draw a long rectangle and divide it into 5 boxes. Make a value scale: leave box 1 white,
make box 5 as dark as you can, and grade the middle boxes evenly. Try to make the jump
between boxes smooth.

**Concept / Mini-Lesson — 7 min**
Shine the light on the ball from one side. Point out the highlight, the gradual shift to
the core shadow, and the cast shadow on the table. Explain: our eyes read these value
changes as "round" and "solid."

**Guided Practice — 6 min**
Draw a circle. Decide where the light is (say top-left). Add a highlight there (leave it
white), build up gray toward the bottom-right for the core shadow, and add a cast shadow
on the opposite side. Blend so there are no harsh jumps.

**Main Activity — 10 min: "Sphere & Friends"**
Set up the real fruit/ball under the light and draw it from observation, shading it to
match what you see. Add at least one more simple form next to it (a cup, a box) and shade
that too, keeping the SAME light source for both.

**Reflection / Share — 2 min**
Does your drawing look like you could pick it up? Where is the light coming from?

## If You Finish Early (+10–15 min)
**Five-Value Portrait of an Object:** Pick any object in the room and shade it using only
the five values from your warm-up scale — no in-between guessing. Squint at the object to
see where the light, medium, and dark areas really are.

## What to look for (teacher check)
- A value scale that goes smoothly from light to dark.
- A consistent light source across the whole drawing.
