# Lesson 2: Shape & Form — Flat vs. Solid

**Elements of Art:** Shape, Form
**Time:** 30 minutes

## Learning goal
The student can tell the difference between a 2D **shape** and a 3D **form**, and can
turn a flat shape into a form by adding value.

## Vocabulary
- **Shape** — flat, 2D, has length and width (circle, square, triangle).
- **Form** — solid, 3D, has length, width, AND depth (sphere, cube, cylinder, cone).
- **Geometric** — math shapes with clear edges (squares, circles).
- **Organic** — free, natural shapes (leaves, puddles, clouds).
- **Geometric → form pairs:** circle→sphere, square→cube, triangle→cone, rectangle→cylinder.

## Materials
- Pencil and eraser
- White paper
- A few real objects to look at: a ball, a box/cube, a can (cylinder), an ice-cream cone if handy

## Lesson plan (30 minutes)

**Warm-Up — 5 min**
Draw 4 geometric shapes (circle, square, triangle, rectangle) and 4 organic shapes
(leaf, cloud, blob, flower). Label which group is which.

**Concept / Mini-Lesson — 7 min**
Hold up the ball and the drawn circle. "What's the difference?" A circle is flat; a
ball (sphere) looks like you could pick it up. The trick that turns a shape into a
form is **shading** — light on one side, dark on the other. Show the 4 shape→form
pairs.

**Guided Practice — 6 min**
Draw a circle, then turn it into a sphere together: pick a light source (say, top-left),
leave that side light, and shade the bottom-right darker. Add a shadow under it.

**Main Activity — 10 min: "Form Collection"**
Draw all four forms — sphere, cube, cylinder, cone — using shading so each looks 3D.
Then arrange them on a table line (a single horizontal line = the table edge) so they
look like they're sitting in a real space.

**Reflection / Share — 2 min**
Which form was easiest to make look 3D? Which was hardest?

## If You Finish Early (+10–15 min)
**Build a Robot or Ice-Cream Sundae:** Combine forms into one object — a robot built
from cubes and cylinders, or a sundae from spheres (scoops) and a cone. Shade every
part so the whole thing looks solid.

## What to look for (teacher check)
- Can the student explain shape vs. form in their own words?
- Did shading actually make the forms look 3D (clear light and dark sides)?
