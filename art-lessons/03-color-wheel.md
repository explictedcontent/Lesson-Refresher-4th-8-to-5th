# Lesson 3: The Color Wheel

**Element of Art:** Color
**Time:** 30 minutes

## Learning goal
The student can build a color wheel and name primary, secondary, and tertiary colors,
and can explain complementary colors.

## Vocabulary
- **Primary colors** — red, yellow, blue. You can't mix them from other colors.
- **Secondary colors** — orange, green, violet (purple). Made by mixing two primaries.
- **Tertiary colors** — red-orange, yellow-orange, yellow-green, blue-green, blue-violet,
  red-violet. Made by mixing a primary with a neighbor secondary.
- **Complementary colors** — colors across from each other on the wheel (red/green,
  blue/orange, yellow/violet). They make each other "pop."

## Materials
- White paper
- Markers, crayons, or paint in at least red, yellow, blue (plus others if you have them)
- Pencil and a round object to trace (cup or lid) for the wheel

## Lesson plan (30 minutes)

**Warm-Up — 5 min**
Quick quiz drawing: write the 3 primary colors. Then guess: red + yellow = ? blue +
yellow = ? red + blue = ? Write your guesses.

**Concept / Mini-Lesson — 7 min**
Explain the three families. Primary colors are the "parents," secondary colors are
their "children," tertiary colors are the "grandchildren." Show how the wheel is
arranged so mixed colors sit between their parents.

**Guided Practice — 6 min**
Trace a circle. Mark 3 spots evenly (like a triangle) for the primaries: red at top,
yellow at bottom-right, blue at bottom-left. Color those in.

**Main Activity — 10 min: "Complete the Color Wheel"**
Fill in the secondary colors between the primaries (orange between red & yellow, green
between yellow & blue, violet between blue & red). If you have enough colors, add the
six tertiary colors in between. You now have a full color wheel reference to keep.

**Reflection / Share — 2 min**
Which two colors sit directly across from each other? (Those are complements.)

## If You Finish Early (+10–15 min)
**Complementary Pop Art:** Pick one pair of complementary colors (e.g., blue & orange).
Draw a simple object (a fish, a flower) and color it using ONLY that pair — object in
one color, background in its complement. Notice how strongly it stands out.

## What to look for (teacher check)
- Correct placement of primary and secondary colors.
- Student can point to a complementary pair.
