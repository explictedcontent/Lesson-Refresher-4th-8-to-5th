# Lesson 12: Proportion & Portraits

**Principle of Design:** Proportion (applied to drawing a face)
**Time:** 30 minutes

## Learning goal
The student can use simple proportion guidelines to draw a face where the features sit in
the right places — fixing the common mistake of putting eyes too high.

## Vocabulary
- **Proportion** — the size relationship between parts (the head to the body, the eyes to
  the face).
- **Guidelines** — light pencil lines that help place features correctly.
- **Symmetry line** — the center line down the middle of the face.
- **Rule of thumb:** the eyes sit about **halfway down** the head — not near the top!

## Materials
- Pencil, eraser, white paper
- A mirror or a photo of a face to look at (optional but helpful)

## Lesson plan (30 minutes)

**Warm-Up — 5 min**
Quick guess: draw a face the way you normally would, fast. Keep it — we'll compare it to
the "measured" face at the end.

**Concept / Mini-Lesson — 7 min**
Most people draw eyes too high. The real proportions of a face:
- Eyes are about **halfway down** the head.
- The bottom of the nose is about halfway between the eyes and the chin.
- The mouth is about a third of the way from the nose to the chin.
- Eyes are about **one eye-width apart**.
- Ears line up roughly between the eyebrows and the bottom of the nose.

**Guided Practice — 6 min**
Draw an oval (egg shape) for the head. Lightly add guidelines: a vertical center line, a
horizontal line halfway down (eye line), then mark the nose line and mouth line below it.

**Main Activity — 10 min: "Proportional Portrait"**
Using the guidelines, draw a full face — eyes on the eye line, nose and mouth on their
lines, ears in place, then add hair. Use a mirror or photo to check. Erase the guidelines
at the end.

**Reflection / Share — 2 min**
Compare the warm-up face to the measured face. What looks more correct now? (Usually the
eye placement.)

## If You Finish Early (+10–15 min)
**Profile or Self-Portrait:** Try drawing the face from the side (profile), OR do a full
self-portrait looking in a mirror, adding value/shading from Lesson 5 to make the face
look round.

## What to look for (teacher check)
- Eyes placed around the halfway line (not at the top of the head).
- Features roughly follow the proportion guidelines.
