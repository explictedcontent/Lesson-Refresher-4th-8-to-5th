# Lesson 11: Emphasis & Focal Point

**Principle of Design:** Emphasis
**Time:** 30 minutes

## Learning goal
The student can create a clear focal point — the spot the eye goes to first — using
contrast in color, size, or isolation.

## Vocabulary
- **Emphasis** — making one part of the artwork stand out the most.
- **Focal point** — the exact spot your eye lands on first.
- **Contrast** — strong differences (light vs. dark, bright vs. dull, big vs. small).
- **Isolation** — separating one object from a group so it draws attention.
- **Color pop** — one bright/colored element in a mostly dull or gray scene.

## Materials
- White paper
- Markers, crayons, or colored pencils (need at least one bright color + grays/dull colors)
- Pencil

## Lesson plan (30 minutes)

**Warm-Up — 5 min**
Draw 9 circles in a grid. Make 8 of them the same and ONE different (a different color, or
much bigger, or the only one filled in). Notice your eye snaps to the odd one — that's
emphasis.

**Concept / Mini-Lesson — 7 min**
Artists *guide* where you look first. Tools for emphasis:
- **Color contrast** — one red flower in a green field.
- **Size** — the biggest thing wins your attention.
- **Isolation** — one object set apart from a crowd.
- **Lines pointing** — roads, fingers, or shadows that aim at the focal point.
Show that without a focal point, the eye wanders and the art feels flat.

**Guided Practice — 6 min**
Draw a small flock of birds, all gray, except make ONE bird bright and slightly bigger.
Watch how it becomes the star.

**Main Activity — 10 min: "Find My Focal Point"**
Create a picture with one obvious focal point. Ideas: a colorful balloon in a gray city, a
single lit window in a dark building, one fish lit up in a dim sea. Use at least two
emphasis tools (e.g., color pop + isolation).

**Reflection / Share — 2 min**
Where does your eye go first? Which trick made that happen?

## If You Finish Early (+10–15 min)
**Black-and-White-Plus-One:** Redraw any scene from an earlier lesson in only grays/black,
then add a single spot of bright color exactly where you want the viewer to look. This
"selective color" technique is used in photography and film posters.

## What to look for (teacher check)
- There is ONE clear focal point (not three things competing).
- Student can name which emphasis tools they used.
