# Lesson 6: Texture — How Things Feel (and Look Like They Feel)

**Element of Art:** Texture
**Time:** 30 minutes

## Learning goal
The student can tell real texture from implied texture and can draw implied textures
that make a flat picture look touchable.

## Vocabulary
- **Texture** — the way a surface feels or looks like it would feel.
- **Real (actual) texture** — texture you can really touch (sandpaper, tree bark).
- **Implied texture** — texture an artist *draws* so it looks rough, fuzzy, or shiny,
  even though the paper is flat.
- **Frottage / rubbing** — placing paper over a textured surface and rubbing with a
  pencil to capture the pattern.

## Materials
- Pencil and white paper
- Crayon (peeled, for rubbings)
- Things with texture around the house: a coin, leaf, brick, screen, fabric, tree bark

## Lesson plan (30 minutes)

**Warm-Up — 5 min: Texture Hunt**
Place paper over 4 different textured surfaces and make a crayon rubbing of each. Label
them. These are captures of REAL texture.

**Concept / Mini-Lesson — 7 min**
Explain the difference: a photo of a cactus *looks* spiky but is smooth to touch — that's
**implied** texture. Show how to draw common textures with marks:
- Fur = short curved strokes in one direction
- Scales = overlapping U-shapes
- Bricks = stacked rectangles with shadow lines
- Shiny metal = sharp white highlights next to dark areas
- Grass/hair = quick flicked lines

**Guided Practice — 6 min**
Make a "texture sampler": divide paper into 4 boxes and draw fur, scales, bark, and
something shiny — implied textures only, no rubbings.

**Main Activity — 10 min: "Texture Creature"**
Invent an animal and give it at least three different drawn textures (for example, furry
body, scaly tail, smooth shiny eyes). The goal is to make a viewer want to reach out and
touch it.

**Reflection / Share — 2 min**
Which texture turned out the most convincing? What marks made it work?

## If You Finish Early (+10–15 min)
**Rubbing Collage:** Cut shapes out of your warm-up rubbings and arrange/glue them into a
new picture (a robot, a landscape, a face) so real textures build a brand-new image.

## What to look for (teacher check)
- Student can explain real vs. implied texture.
- At least three clearly different textures in the creature drawing.
