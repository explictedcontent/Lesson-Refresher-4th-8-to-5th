# Lesson 7: Space — Positive, Negative, Near & Far

**Element of Art:** Space
**Time:** 30 minutes

## Learning goal
The student can identify positive and negative space and use simple depth tricks
(overlap, size, placement) to show foreground, middle ground, and background.

## Vocabulary
- **Positive space** — the object itself.
- **Negative space** — the empty space around and between objects.
- **Foreground** — the part of the scene closest to you (drawn big, low on the page).
- **Middle ground** — the middle of the scene.
- **Background** — the part farthest away (drawn small, high on the page).
- **Overlap** — when one object covers part of another; the front one looks closer.

## Materials
- Pencil and white paper
- Black marker
- A pair of scissors and a second sheet (for the early-finisher activity)

## Lesson plan (30 minutes)

**Warm-Up — 5 min**
Draw your hand by tracing it — but instead of coloring the hand, color in everything
*around* it (the negative space). This shows how negative space can define a shape.

**Concept / Mini-Lesson — 7 min**
Explain the three depth tricks artists use to make flat paper look deep:
1. **Size** — closer things are bigger, far things are smaller.
2. **Placement** — closer things sit lower on the page, far things sit higher.
3. **Overlap** — closer things cover part of the things behind them.

**Guided Practice — 6 min**
Draw three trees: one big and low (foreground), one medium (middle ground), one small and
high (background). Overlap them slightly. Add a horizon line. Already it looks like a
field stretching away.

**Main Activity — 10 min: "Deep Scene"**
Create a landscape with a clear foreground, middle ground, and background using all three
depth tricks. Ideas: a road going into mountains, a beach to the horizon, a city street.

**Reflection / Share — 2 min**
Point to the foreground and background. What made the far part look far away?

## If You Finish Early (+10–15 min)
**Notan Cut-Out:** Fold a square of paper in half. Cut shapes from the fold and unfold the
pieces, flipping them outward and gluing them onto a contrasting paper. The cut-out
(positive) and the hole (negative) mirror each other — a classic design exercise in
positive/negative space.

## What to look for (teacher check)
- Student can point out positive vs. negative space.
- The landscape uses at least two of the three depth tricks.
