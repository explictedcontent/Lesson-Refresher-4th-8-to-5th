# Lesson 4: Color Moods — Warm, Cool, Tints & Shades

**Element of Art:** Color (continued)
**Time:** 30 minutes

## Learning goal
The student can sort colors into warm and cool families, make tints and shades, and
choose colors to set a mood.

## Vocabulary
- **Warm colors** — red, orange, yellow. Feel hot, energetic, close (fire, sun).
- **Cool colors** — blue, green, violet. Feel calm, quiet, far away (water, ice).
- **Tint** — a color + white (lighter). Pink is a tint of red.
- **Shade** — a color + black (darker). Maroon is a shade of red.
- **Mood** — the feeling a color choice gives the viewer.

## Materials
- White paper
- Paint, markers, or colored pencils (need a way to lighten/darken — white & black, or
  press hard/soft with colored pencil)

## Lesson plan (30 minutes)

**Warm-Up — 5 min**
Make two columns: WARM and COOL. List every color you can think of in the right column.
Then circle which list reminds you of a campfire and which reminds you of a swimming pool.

**Concept / Mini-Lesson — 7 min**
Warm colors seem to come toward you; cool colors seem to back away — artists use this to
show distance. Then show tints and shades: same color, made lighter (tint) or darker
(shade). This is how you show light and depth without changing the color itself.

**Guided Practice — 6 min**
Pick one color. Make a strip: shade (darkest) → pure color → tint (lightest). Three to
five steps. This is called a **value scale** for that color.

**Main Activity — 10 min: "Two Moods, One Scene"**
Draw the same simple scene twice (small). Color one version with ONLY warm colors and
one with ONLY cool colors. A house, a tree, the sky. Notice how the warm one feels sunny
and the cool one feels like night or winter.

**Reflection / Share — 2 min**
Which mood did each color family create? Which do you like better and why?

## If You Finish Early (+10–15 min)
**Monochromatic Challenge:** Pick ONE color and draw a picture using only its tints and
shades (light to dark) — no other colors allowed. This is called a *monochromatic*
artwork and it teaches you to control value.

## What to look for (teacher check)
- Correct sorting of warm vs. cool.
- A visible difference between the tint and the shade in their value strip.
