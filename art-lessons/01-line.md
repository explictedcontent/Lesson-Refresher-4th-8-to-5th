# Lesson 1: Line — The Building Block of Art

**Element of Art:** Line
**Time:** 30 minutes
**Grade level:** 4th → 5th refresher

## Learning goal
The student can name different types of lines and use them on purpose to show
movement, feeling, and direction.

## Vocabulary
- **Line** — a mark made by a moving point; the path between two points.
- **Horizontal** — left to right (calm, restful).
- **Vertical** — up and down (strong, tall).
- **Diagonal** — slanted (action, movement).
- **Zigzag** — sharp turns (energy, excitement).
- **Curved / spiral** — smooth bends (gentle, flowing).
- **Implied line** — a line your eye follows even when it isn't fully drawn.

## Materials
- Pencil and eraser
- Black fine-tip marker
- Plain white paper (2 sheets)

## Lesson plan (30 minutes)

**Warm-Up — 5 min**
Fold one sheet of paper into 6 boxes. In each box, draw a different kind of line:
horizontal, vertical, diagonal, zigzag, curved, and spiral. Label each one.

**Concept / Mini-Lesson — 7 min**
Explain that *every* drawing starts with lines. Lines can show feelings:
- Calm water = horizontal lines.
- A tall skyscraper = vertical lines.
- A bolt of lightning = zigzag lines.
- A rolling hill = curved lines.
Ask: "What kind of line would you use to draw something scary? Something peaceful?"

**Guided Practice — 6 min**
Together, draw a simple landscape using ONLY lines — no coloring. Use horizontal
lines for the ground, curved lines for hills, zigzags for mountains, spirals for
clouds or wind.

**Main Activity — 10 min: "Line Feelings"**
On the second sheet, divide it in half. On the left, draw a picture that feels
**calm and peaceful** using gentle lines. On the right, draw a picture that feels
**busy and exciting** using sharp, active lines. Same scene if you want (e.g., a
calm sea vs. a stormy sea).

**Reflection / Share — 2 min**
Which side feels more exciting, and which lines made it that way?

## If You Finish Early (+10–15 min)
**Line-Only Animal Challenge:** Pick an animal and draw it using only one type of
line (for example, an entire cat made from curved lines only, or a porcupine made
from zigzags). Then trace your favorite drawing of the day with the black marker to
make it bold.

## What to look for (teacher check)
- Can the student name at least 5 line types?
- Did they choose lines on purpose to match a feeling?
