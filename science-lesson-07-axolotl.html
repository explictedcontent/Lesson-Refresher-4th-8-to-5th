The Animal That Rebuilds Itself

What Is an Axolotl?

The axolotl is an amphibian — a kind of salamander. But it is a very strange salamander. Most amphibians go through a big change called metamorphosis: a frog starts as an egg, hatches into a tadpole that lives in water, then transforms into a froglet, and finally becomes an adult frog that can hop on land. The axolotl never makes that change. It stays in its baby, water-living form for its whole life. Scientists call this "staying forever young" neoteny.

The Famous Smile

Axolotls look like they are always smiling. That "smile" is really just the shape of their wide mouth. Add their tiny eyes and the feathery gills that fan out from their head, and you get one of the most adorable faces in the animal world.

The Regeneration Superpower

Here is the axolotl's most amazing power. If a predator bites off one of its legs, the axolotl does not just heal over — it grows the whole leg back. First, cells at the wound turn back into special stem-like cells that form a bump called a blastema. From that blastema, a brand-new limb grows — with all its bone, muscle, skin, and nerves — and there is no scar at all.

How Much Can It Regrow?

Legs are only the beginning. Axolotls can regrow their limbs, tail, gills, parts of the heart, the spinal cord, and even parts of the brain. Almost no other animal on Earth can do this.

Why Scientists Care

When a human gets a deep cut, we heal by making scar tissue — we do not grow the body part back. If scientists can figure out the axolotl's trick, doctors might one day help humans heal spinal injuries or regrow damaged tissue. That is why the axolotl's huge genome — about ten times bigger than a human's — is being studied so carefully.

How Do They Breathe?

Axolotls have a triple breathing system. They use their feathery gills to pull oxygen from the water, they can gulp air at the surface, and they can even absorb oxygen straight through their skin.

Cold-Blooded and Colorful

Axolotls are cold-blooded, which means their body temperature matches the temperature of the water around them. In the wild, they are usually dark brown or green so they can hide, or camouflage, in muddy canals. The famous pink axolotls (called leucistic) are mostly bred by people in captivity.

Predator and Prey

Axolotls are hunters. They eat worms, insect larvae, and small fish, slurping their prey in with a sudden burst of suction, like a living vacuum. And even though they stay in their larval form forever, adult axolotls can still reproduce and lay eggs.
