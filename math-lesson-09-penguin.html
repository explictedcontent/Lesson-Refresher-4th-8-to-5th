🧭 Phase 2 — The Number Line (8 min)

Integers are the whole numbers plus their negatives — and zero right in the middle.

📋 Positive, Negative & Zero

Positive
right of zero
Zero

Negative
left of zero
Farther RIGHT always means a bigger number. Farther LEFT always means a smaller number. With negatives it feels backwards: as the digit gets bigger, the number gets smaller. That's why −9 < −2 — negative nine is farther left, so it's less. Any negative is always less than 0, and 0 is always less than any positive.

➕ Phase 3 — Adding & Subtracting (10 min)

Use the number line: adding moves you RIGHT, subtracting moves you LEFT — even across zero.

📋 Two Worked Examples

Adding: −3 + 5 = ?
Start at −3 Move RIGHT 5 (because we add 5) −3 → −2 → −1 → 0 → 1 → 2
You started below zero and moved right past it. Landing spot: 2.
Subtracting: 4 − 7 = ?
Start at 4 Move LEFT 7 (because we subtract 7) 4 → 3 → 2 → 1 → 0 → −1 → −2 → −3
You started above zero and moved left through it into the negatives: −3.
Add → move right. Subtract → move left. Adding a negative, like −2 + (−4), is the same as moving left again, so it goes even lower: −6. Type answers with a minus sign for negatives, like −7.
