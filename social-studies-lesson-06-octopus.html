Core Reading: The Octopus's World

Read carefully — the quiz and activities come from this passage.

If you dropped a submarine into any ocean on Earth — the Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic, or Southern — you could find an octopus. Octopuses live in every ocean, from warm tropical coral reefs to the freezing waters near the poles. Most make their habitat on the shallow continental shelf, the underwater edge of a continent, where food is easy to find.

Habitat: the natural home of an animal.   Continental shelf: the shallow, underwater edge of a continent.   Hemisphere: one half of the Earth.   Aquaculture: farming animals or plants in water.

The biggest of them all is the giant Pacific octopus, which can weigh over 100 pounds! It lives along the North Pacific rim — the coastal waters of Japan, Alaska, British Columbia, and California. That range stretches across the entire northern Pacific Ocean, in the Northern Hemisphere. The common octopus lives in the Mediterranean Sea and the Atlantic Ocean, where scientists have studied its remarkable intelligence since the days of ancient Greece. And in 1998, divers off Indonesia discovered the amazing mimic octopus, which impersonates lionfish, sea snakes, and flatfish to fool predators!

The ocean is divided into depth zones. Most octopuses live in the sunlight zone (0–650 feet) and the twilight zone (650–3,300 feet). But the ghostly dumbo octopus, named for its ear-like fins, lives about 13,000 feet down in the pitch-black midnight zone — deeper than almost any other octopus.

Octopuses matter to people, too. For thousands of years, octopus has been an important part of the food and culture of Japan, Korea, Spain, Greece, and Italy. And one octopus became world-famous: in 2016, Inky the octopus escaped from the National Aquarium of New Zealand. He squeezed out of his tank, slid across the floor, and slipped down a drain pipe that led straight back to the sea. Nobody ever saw him again!

Today, octopuses face threats from overfishing and habitat loss. When the world's first octopus farm was proposed — a kind of aquaculture — it sparked a worldwide debate: how should such intelligent animals be treated? Scientists, chefs, and kids around the world are still discussing that question.
