🌅 Phase 1 — Warm-Up (5 min)

4 quick questions about WHY math needs an agreed order — and what that order is.

📋 Why We Need a Rule at All

Look at this: 2 + 3 × 4

If you add first, you get 5 × 4 = 20. If you multiply first, you get 2 + 12 = 14. Same expression, two different answers!

That would be a disaster — a builder and an engineer reading the same plan would get different numbers. So mathematicians all over the world agreed on one order. Everyone follows it, so everyone gets the same answer. (For 2 + 3 × 4, the agreed answer is 14.)

🔎 Phase 2 — The Step Solver (12 min)

📋 The Agreed Order

1
Parentheses ( ) — do everything inside them first, no matter where they sit in the problem.
2
Multiply & Divide — these two are the same level. Do them in the order you meet them, working left to right.
3
Add & Subtract — also the same level as each other. Again, work left to right.
The famous memory trick: PEMDAS — “Please Excuse My Dear Aunt Sally” = Parentheses, Exponents, Multiply, Divide, Add, Subtract. (Exponents are the little raised numbers like 5², which you will meet later — this lesson sticks to parentheses, ×, ÷, +, −.)
⚠️ The #1 mistake with PEMDAS. The letters make it look like M beats D, and A beats S. That is not true. Multiply and divide are tied — so are add and subtract. When two tied operations are both waiting, you do the one that is further LEFT first.
12 ÷ 3 × 2 → the ÷ is further left, so 12 ÷ 3 = 4, then 4 × 2 = 8. (Doing 3 × 2 first would wrongly give 2.)
10 − 4 + 3 → the − is further left, so 10 − 4 = 6, then 6 + 3 = 9. (Doing 4 + 3 first would wrongly give 3.)

🔗 Phase 3 — Parentheses Power (12 min)

Parentheses are a "do me first" sign. Move them and the answer changes.

📋 Same Numbers, Different Answer

No parentheses
2 + 3 × 4
= 2 + 12
= 14
With parentheses
(2 + 3) × 4
= 5 × 4
= 20

Exactly the same numbers and signs — but the parentheses promoted the addition to the front of the line, and the answer jumped from 14 to 20. That is why writers of math problems use parentheses: they let you choose what happens first.

Two traps to watch for.
1. Parentheses come first even when they are at the end of the expression: in 20 − (3 + 5) you still do 3 + 5 first.
2. Once the inside of the parentheses becomes one number, the parentheses have done their job and simply disappear: (2 + 3) × 4 → 5 × 4.
Nested parentheses? If one pair sits inside another, start with the innermost pair and work your way out. You will see more of that in 5th grade.
