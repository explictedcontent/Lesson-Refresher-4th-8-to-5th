Core Reading: Creatures of the Night Around the World

Read carefully — the quiz and activities come from this passage.

When the sun goes down and most animals are settling in to sleep, another kind of animal is just waking up. Bats are nocturnal — they are active at night — and they are one of the most misunderstood creatures on Earth. Here is the fact that surprises most people: bats are the only mammals that can truly fly. Flying squirrels and other "flying" animals only glide from tree to tree, but a bat flaps its wings and flies like a bird.

There are over 1,400 species of bats. That is an enormous number — about 1 in every 5 mammal species on Earth is a bat! They live on every continent except Antarctica, making their homes in deserts, rainforests, mountains, and even busy cities.

Mammal: a warm-blooded animal that feeds its babies milk.   Species: one particular kind of living thing.   Nocturnal: awake and active at night.   Pollinate: to move pollen so plants can make fruit and seeds.   Conservation: protecting animals and nature so they survive.

Most bats are helpful to people, even if we rarely see them. The great majority eat insects — a single bat can gobble up thousands of mosquitoes in one night. That makes bats a kind of natural pest control that helps farmers protect their crops without extra chemicals. Other bats, called fruit bats or flying foxes, drink flower nectar and eat fruit. As they feed, they pollinate flowers and spread seeds, helping rainforests grow. Foods like bananas, mangoes, and agave (the plant used to make tequila) all depend on bats.

So what about vampire bats? Out of all 1,400 species, only 3 are "vampire bats," and they live in Latin America. They feed mostly on the blood of animals like cattle — not on people. The scary reputation of bats mostly comes from stories, not from real life.

Bats and people share the world in interesting ways. Bracken Cave in Texas is home to millions of bats — one of the largest gatherings of animals anywhere on Earth. At dusk, crowds of people come to watch the bats stream out of the cave into the evening sky. Bats also mean different things in different cultures. In Chinese tradition, the bat is a symbol of good luck and happiness. In Western Halloween traditions, bats are linked with spookiness and haunted houses. It is the same animal, but different cultures see it in completely different ways.

Today many bats need our help. A disease called white-nose syndrome and the loss of their habitats threaten many species. People are working on conservation — building "bat houses" for them to roost in and protecting the caves where they sleep. The more we learn about bats, the more we see they are helpers, not monsters.
