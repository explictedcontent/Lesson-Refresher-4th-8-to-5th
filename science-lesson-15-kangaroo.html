Phase 2 Core Reading
10 min · Read carefully — quiz questions come from this passage

Built to Bounce

A Very Different Mammal

The kangaroo is a marsupial — a mammal whose babies are born tiny and finish growing up inside the mother's pouch. Like all mammals, a kangaroo is warm-blooded, so it makes its own body heat instead of relying on the sun. A baby kangaroo is called a joey, and the way it grows up is one of the strangest and most amazing stories in the animal world.

The Tiny Joey

A newborn joey is born about the size of a jellybean — blind and hairless and smaller than your thumb. Right away it does something incredible: it crawls all the way up to the pouch by itself, then latches on and drinks milk for months as it grows. Later the joey hops in and out of the pouch to explore, climbing back in for safety, until one day it is simply too big to fit.

The Springy Legs

Kangaroos move by hopping, which scientists call saltation. They are the largest animals that get around by hopping. Their huge back legs have stretchy tendons that act like springs, storing and releasing energy with every bounce. Because of this, hopping fast actually costs less energy the faster they go — the springs do a lot of the work for free.

The Amazing Tail

A kangaroo's tail is long and muscular. When the kangaroo is hopping, the tail works like a balance bar to keep it steady. When it moves slowly, the tail becomes a fifth "leg", pushing the kangaroo forward like an extra limb. Those powerful hind feet give the push for giant leaps, and they can also deliver a strong defensive kick.

Staying Cool in the Outback

The Australian outback gets very hot, so kangaroos have a clever trick: they lick their forearms, where the blood vessels run close to the skin. As the spit evaporates, it cools the blood underneath — a built-in air conditioner. Kangaroos are herbivores that graze on grass, mostly in the cooler morning and evening, and they can go a long time on very little water.

Mobs and Boomers

Kangaroos live together in groups called mobs, which helps keep them safe from danger. The biggest, strongest males are called boomers. One last odd fact: because of their big feet and heavy tail, kangaroos cannot easily hop backward — they are built to keep moving forward.
