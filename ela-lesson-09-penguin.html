Reading Passage

The Long Night

On the great white plain of Antarctica, a young emperor penguin named Tumi stood very, very still. Balanced carefully on top of his feet, tucked warm beneath a special flap of his belly, was his greatest treasure: his very first egg. He could not see it, but he could feel its gentle weight, and he would not let it touch the frozen ground for even a second.

The long polar night was beginning — months and months with no sun at all. The sky faded from gray to deep, endless dark. The wind rose into a relentless blizzard that screamed across the ice and stung Tumi's eyes. He was hungry. He had not eaten in weeks, and there would be no food until the sea thawed. Still, he could not leave. If he stepped away, the egg would freeze in minutes.

"You look frightened, little one," said a deep voice. It was Berg, an older penguin who had guarded many eggs. "The night is long. But you will not face it alone."

"How does anyone survive this?" Tumi whispered. "The cold never stops."

Berg nodded toward the center of the colony, where thousands of penguins pressed together in a great dark shelter of feathers and warmth. "See the huddle? Alone, we freeze. Together, we endure. Come — take your place."

Tumi shuffled forward, still balancing his egg, and squeezed into the crowd. At first he stood on the freezing outer edge, where the wind bit hardest. But slowly, penguin by penguin, the huddle rotated. Those on the cold outside shuffled inward, and those in the warm middle took their turn facing the storm. This cooperation kept them all alive — nobody stayed out in the cold forever, and nobody hogged the warm center.

Still, the long night tested Tumi's endurance like nothing ever had. Days blurred into weeks of darkness. His legs ached. His belly growled. More than once he wanted to give up. But each time, he felt the tiny weight resting on his feet, and something deep inside him — an instinct older than any storm — told him to hold on. His devotion to that small life would not let him quit.

Then, one still morning, he felt it: a tiny tapping from beneath his belly. Crack. The egg was hatching! A damp, fuzzy gray head poked out, and two bright eyes blinked up at him. A chick! It peeped, and Tumi's heart nearly burst.

At almost the very same moment, a thin band of gold appeared at the edge of the world. The sun — the sun was returning! And waddling across the ice came Tumi's mate, home at last from the distant sea, her belly full of food for their new chick.

Tumi was exhausted, thinner than he had ever been. But as he looked from the sunrise to his mate to the peeping chick on his feet, he felt triumphant. He had endured the longest night of his life — and his love had carried them all the way through to the light.

Comprehension Check
8 min

Tap each question to reveal the answer. Think about your answer first!

1. What is Tumi's job during the long night? ▶
To guard and keep his single egg warm, balanced on his feet under his belly flap, all winter.
2. Why can't Tumi leave to find food? ▶
If he leaves, the egg would freeze in minutes; he must stay even though he's starving.
3. What does Berg teach Tumi about surviving the cold? ▶
To join the huddle — "Alone, we freeze. Together, we endure" — penguins share warmth by taking turns.
4. What two good things happen at the end? ▶
The egg hatches into a chick, and Tumi's mate returns from the sea with food as the sun comes back.
5. What is the THEME (big lesson) of the story? ▶
Perseverance and sacrifice — enduring great hardship out of love and devotion.
