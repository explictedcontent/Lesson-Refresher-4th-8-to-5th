Reading Passage
10 min

Nimbus Learns to Soar

Nimbus was a young sky bison who had never flown far from home. Each morning he grazed in the green valley where he was born, and each night he curled up beside his rider, a kind airbender kid named Kaya. The two of them shared a bond so close that Kaya could feel what Nimbus was feeling just by resting a hand on his soft fur.

One morning a message arrived by hawk. A village across the mountains needed medicine, and only a sky bison could reach it fast enough. But the journey was long, and the tall peaks looked daunting. Nimbus was hesitant — he had never crossed the tall mountains, and everyone said the wind up high was fierce.

"We'll go together," Kaya said gently, scratching his ear. "Trust the wind, and trust yourself." Nimbus took a deep breath, spread his six legs, and pushed off the ground. Up, up they climbed, the valley shrinking below them like a green coin.

As they rose toward the peaks, a strong gust slammed into Nimbus and threw them off course. He panicked, tumbling sideways through the cold air. "Stay calm!" Kaya called over the roar of the wind. "Spread your wings wide — feel for the warm air rising up!" Nimbus stopped fighting. He opened his great body to the sky and, sure enough, a warm current called a thermal caught him and lifted him gently upward.

From then on, Nimbus learned to be patient. Instead of struggling against the wind, he let it carry him, tilting and drifting the way the air wanted him to go. His flying grew steady and sure. Kaya laughed with joy as they glided between the snowy peaks like a leaf on a river.

They reached the village just in time. People rushed out of their homes, cheering and waving, as Kaya lowered the medicine down to them. Nimbus felt triumphant and proud of what he and Kaya had done together.

On the flight home, Nimbus realized something. Courage did not mean he had felt no fear. He had been scared the whole time. Courage meant flying on anyway — with a friend beside him, and a little trust in the wind. That, he understood, was what carried them through hard journeys.
